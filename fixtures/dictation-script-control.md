## Why a control exists

Everything measured so far compared engines on **domain** vocabulary and found
them wanting. That result is only meaningful if the engines are otherwise fine
on this voice, this microphone and this room.

Harvard Sentences are deliberately *not* domain vocabulary. They are common
words chosen for phonetic balance. So:

- **Both engines near-perfect here, poor on a domain script** → the vocabulary
  is the problem. That is the project's central claim, and this is what makes it
  falsifiable.
- **Both engines poor here too** → the problem is the recording setup, the
  accent, or the microphone, and the domain results say much less than they
  appear to.

Read the control on any machine, microphone or voice before trusting a domain
comparison from it. It costs thirty seconds.

## What this deliberately does not test

Harvard Sentences carry no jargon, no proper nouns, no acronyms and no British
or American spelling ambiguity. They cannot tell you anything about the problem
Macalla exists to solve. That is the point of a control: it isolates everything
*except* the variable under test.
